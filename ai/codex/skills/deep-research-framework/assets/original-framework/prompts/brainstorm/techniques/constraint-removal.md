# Ideation Technique: Constraint Removal

## Objective
Generate ideas by systematically identifying and temporarily removing constraints, exploring unconstrained solution spaces, then reintroducing constraints to find practical compromises.

## Seed Problem
[Populate from `context/from-human/brainstorm-context.md`]

## Process

### Step 1: Constraint Inventory
List all constraints — both explicit and implicit:

**Explicit constraints** (stated in the problem definition):
- [e.g., budget limit, hardware requirement, timeline]

**Implicit constraints** (assumed but not stated):
- [e.g., "must use existing infrastructure", "must be real-time", "must follow convention X"]

**Self-imposed constraints** (habits or biases):
- [e.g., "we always do it this way", "this technology is the standard"]

### Step 2: One-by-One Removal
For each constraint, ask:
- "What if this constraint didn't exist?"
- "What solution would be ideal in a world without this limitation?"
- Generate 1-2 unconstrained ideas per removed constraint

### Step 3: Constraint Reintroduction
For the most promising unconstrained ideas:
- Gradually reintroduce constraints
- Find the point where the idea remains valuable despite constraints
- Identify creative compromises or workarounds

### Step 4: Multi-Constraint Removal
Try removing 2-3 constraints simultaneously:
- What entirely new solution spaces open up?
- Which constraint combinations are worth challenging?

## Expected Output
- A table with columns: #, Constraint Removed, Unconstrained Idea, Practical Compromise, Direction Tag
- Minimum 8 ideas across at least 4 different constraints
- Round statistics: idea count, new directions, duplication rate
