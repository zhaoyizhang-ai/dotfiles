# Ideation Technique: Random Stimulus

## Objective
Generate ideas by introducing unrelated concepts, technologies, or domains and forcing unexpected connections with the seed problem.

## Process

### Step 1: Generate Random Stimuli
Select 5 random stimuli from diverse categories:
1. A random technology (e.g., blockchain, CRISPR, origami folding)
2. A random natural phenomenon (e.g., murmuration, coral growth, fermentation)
3. A random industry (e.g., fashion, agriculture, space exploration)
4. A random everyday object (e.g., zipper, traffic light, umbrella)
5. A random historical event or concept (e.g., the printing press, bazaar economy, cathedral architecture)

### Step 2: Forced Connection
For each stimulus, force a connection to the seed problem:
- "What if our solution worked like [stimulus]?"
- "What principle from [stimulus] could apply here?"
- "What would a [stimulus]-inspired approach look like?"

Spend at least 2-3 minutes of deep thought per stimulus. Do not dismiss connections prematurely.

### Step 3: Idea Extraction
From each forced connection:
- Extract 1-3 concrete ideas
- Note which property of the stimulus inspired the idea
- Tag with a direction label

### Step 4: Cross-Stimulus Synthesis
Look across the stimulus-inspired ideas:
- Can any two stimulus ideas be combined?
- Do patterns emerge across unrelated stimuli?

## Expected Output
- A table with columns: #, Random Stimulus, Connection Principle, Generated Idea, Direction Tag
- Minimum 8 ideas across at least 4 different stimuli
- Round statistics: idea count, new directions, duplication rate
